4. Head-Only Differentially Private Fine-Tuning

End-to-end DP-SGD at ε = 1 produced a model at chance level. The resolution was to pretrain both encoders non-privately, freeze them, and apply DP-SGD to the fusion head alone.

RecipeNoise scale σTest AUCRound time
End-to-end DP-SGD11.32810.5621046 s

Interpretation: the noise scale is identical in both rows, so noise was never the limiting quantity. What changed is the optimisation geometry — a near-linear probe over frozen pretrained features tolerates σ ≈ 11.3, while training from scratch does not. Rounds also run approximately twenty times faster. The privatised head is 560,645 of 2,157,766 parameters, or 26.0%.

8. Privacy-Unit / k-Scaling Experiment

This is the main scientific experiment. Here k denotes the number of records contributed per patient. The observed record-level versus patient-level utility gap increases after k = 1, while the patient arm remains approximately flat.

k (records/patient)Record/sample arm AUPRCPatient arm AUPRCGapp-value
10.50150.5067−0.00520.75
20.55900.5084+0.05070.0017
40.59240.5092+0.08320.0015
80.59550.5089+0.08660.0049

Interpretation: k = 1 provides a built-in null control, because at one record per patient the record and the patient are the same privacy unit and the two arms must agree. For k > 1 the record-level arm rises while the patient-level arm stays approximately flat, so the widening gap reflects the record arm improving rather than the patient arm degrading. Extra records per patient buy the patient arm nothing, because the noise scales with each patient's total contribution. This supports an observed records-per-patient dependence; external validation is still required before calling it a universal law.

Reporting limits: AUROC follows the same monotone shape (+0.0006, +0.0094, +0.0133, +0.0132) but no value of k reaches significance at three seeds, so AUROC is reported as directionally consistent and not as a resolved effect. Accuracy is pinned at 0.8005 in every patient row, which reflects an uncalibrated 0.5 threshold on imbalanced data, and should not be quoted.

10. Membership-Inference Audit

The original MIA hypothesis predicted a substantially larger patient-level attack advantage for record-level DP. The paired LiRA results did not establish that large effect. A positive control demonstrated that the attack can detect leakage where leakage exists.

ArmMIA AUCOriginal interpretation
Non-private control0.6277Positive control: attack signal present
Record-level DP, ε ≈ 10.5477Small signal; large predicted effect not established
Patient-level DP, ε ≈ 10.5224Near chance; large predicted effect not established
Attack variantPaired Δ (record − patient)95% confidence intervalp-value
Loss threshold+0.0138[−0.0034, +0.0310]0.090
LiRA offline+0.0325[−0.0280, +0.0929]0.210

Interpretation: every confidence interval includes zero. The predicted effect of approximately 0.20 lies far outside all four intervals and is refuted. A small effect is not excluded, since the half-width on the strongest attack is ±0.060 at five seeds. The correct wording is that the large predicted effect is refuted while a small one remains unresolved at this sample size. A supporting diagnostic is that on the patient arm LiRA adds nothing over a plain loss threshold (−0.0071), which is the signature of a defence with no difficulty-confounded signal left rather than of an underpowered adversary.

11. MIA Difficulty-Floor Control

A new control corrects an important evaluation issue: members and non-members differ in intrinsic difficulty. A shadow-only attacker without the target model establishes a difficulty floor. Attack performance should therefore be interpreted relative to this floor rather than only against AUC = 0.5.

ArmAttack AUCDifficulty floorMargin over floor
Positive control0.62540.5626+0.0627
Record DP, ε ≈ 10.54770.5252+0.0226
Patient DP, ε ≈ 10.52240.5167+0.0057
Record DP, ε ≈ 160.54650.5743−0.0278
Patient DP, ε ≈ 160.56300.5259+0.0371

Interpretation: the positive control still clears its difficulty floor, supporting the validity of the attack. The ε ≈ 1 private arms clear their floors only slightly, and the ε ≈ 16 record arm falls below its floor. This makes the refutation of the originally predicted large MIA effect stronger. Members are drawn from the released training patients and non-members from the released test patients, and those pools differ in intrinsic difficulty (mean statistic 10.25 against 7.71), so an attack is otherwise rewarded for detecting difficulty rather than membership. Floors are computed per arm from that arm's own shadow models, which is why they differ; better-fitting models separate easy from hard patients more sharply.

12. Membership Inference at a Loosened Budget

A final test of whether the membership hypothesis failed only because ε = 1 left no headroom for a gap to appear. At ε = 16 the noise scale is thirteen times lower (σ 11.33 falling to 0.85), so a membership signal should survive if one exists.

Attack variantPaired Δ (record − patient)95% confidence intervalReading
Loss threshold+0.0163[+0.0123, +0.0204]Significant, predicted direction, but an eighth of the predicted size
LiRA offline−0.1788[−0.2553, −0.1024]Significant in the reversed direction; an artefact of over-correction
LiRA online+0.0057[−0.0137, +0.0252]Chance
LiRA online, global variance−0.0552[−0.0896, −0.0208]Significant in the reversed direction

Interpretation: the four attacks contradict one another. Two report a significant gap in the direction opposite to the prediction, one reports a significant gap an eighth of the predicted size, and the principled two-sided variant reports chance. Mutually inconsistent estimators are the signature of no underlying signal, with each estimator driven by a different facet of the difficulty confound. Decisively, the record-level arm sits 0.0278 below its own difficulty floor, so it carries no membership signal for a gap to be composed of; loosening the budget sixteenfold changed its attack AUC by 0.0012. The hypothesis that the effect was merely hidden at ε = 1 is therefore refuted, and the question is closed rather than left open at small sample size.

Note for reporting: the LiRA offline value of −0.1788 must be presented together with its explanation. It arises because the record arm's offline score is 0.3842, far below chance, produced by over-correction on an asymmetric difficulty tail. Presented without that explanation it would be misread as evidence that patient-level privacy leaks more.

These arms had never produced a result until four silent defects were fixed on 16 August 2026: a dead command-line argument rejected by the parser, a quantiser with a hardcoded 8-bit path, a summary reading test columns from a file containing only validation columns, and a verdict threshold set at 1e-4.

15. Communication and Compression

MeasurementBytes per roundInterpretation
Full-model FP32 FedAvg258,931,920Reference
Head-only, uncompressed67,277,400Effect of freezing the encoders
Observed, after defect fix16,821,30090.82% reduction against the reference

Interpretation: a defect was found and corrected during this work. The compressor took a fixed fraction of every tensor, so frozen encoders, whose delta is exactly zero, still contributed 20% of their elements to each payload as explicit zeros. Omitting all-zero tensors accounted for a 78.87% reduction on its own. The fix was proven not to alter learning: the dense path is bit-identical to the previous implementation over five rounds, and an isolated re-run moved AUC by 0.0001.

16. Inference Latency and Edge Deployment

PrecisionProviderModel sizeTotal P95Status
FP32CPU10.64 MB16.363 msPass; recommended
FP32GPU (CUDA)10.64 MB22.476 msPass
INT8CPU6.55 MB31.028 msPass but slower than FP32
INT8GPU (CUDA)6.55 MB73.150 msPass but substantially slower

Interpretation: the privacy apparatus is free at serving time. The conformal set construction that carries the coverage guarantee occupies 0.68% of the inference path, and the private mechanism occupies none of it, because its noise is applied to gradients during training. The objection that a privacy apparatus is too heavy for a latency-bound deployment is therefore answered by measurement rather than by argument.

INT8 quantisation is a characterised negative result. It is 1.90 times slower on CPU and 3.25 times slower on GPU than FP32, and misses the 4 MB size target at 6.55 MB. Dynamic quantisation accelerates only matrix-multiply operations, while this model's cost is dominated by convolutions and a recurrent path; and on CUDA the quantised operations have no GPU kernel and fall back to CPU, with the runtime reporting 3180 memory-copy nodes inserted into the graph. The FP32 artifact is the one to deploy.

17. Feature Attribution

Integrated gradients over released checkpoints, twenty runs across five recipes and two baselines, with a separate seed-stability check.

RecipeShare of attribution carried by missingness masks
Full-model DP28.2%
Patient-level DP22.0%
Record-level DP21.6%
Full-model, no DP19.9%
Head-only, no DP15.8%

Interpretation: differential privacy shifts the model's attention toward missingness. Under noise the model relies more on which measurements exist than on their values, and the shift is monotone in how much privacy is applied. Rank correlation remains 0.95 between the head-only private and non-private recipes. For the related claim that missingness carries signal, masks account for 22.0% of attribution mass over five seeds and one observed indicator reaches 4.99% ± 0.33, ranking in the top five on four of five seeds; this should be described as consistently among the strongest rather than assigned a fixed rank, and requires a mask ablation before it constitutes proof.
