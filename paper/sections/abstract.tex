\begin{abstract}
Clinical multimodal Internet-of-Things data cannot be centralized;
hierarchical federated learning addresses that but supplies no privacy
guarantee. Such systems guarantee privacy over records, while clinical
practice requires it over individuals; the two differ wherever an episode of
care yields many records. We present a three-tier framework
whose privacy unit is the patient: gradients are grouped by individual before
bounding, noise is calibrated against every client so the edge tier enters the
budget, and the conformal quantile is charged to it. Across two deployments
the composed budget is $\varepsilon \le 1$, uplink falls by at least
$90.8\,\%$, energy per round by at least $72.3\,\%$, and $95$th-percentile
inference latency is at most $3.0$~ms; collapsing the edge tier does not
improve accuracy, so the savings cost no utility. Pretrained initialization,
not head-only noise, makes a strict budget survivable: tightening
$\varepsilon$ from $\infty$ to $0.5$ costs a randomly initialized arm $0.118$
in area under the ROC curve and a pretrained one nothing measurable;
restricting noise to the head adds nothing at any budget. The price of the
guarantee grows with the records each individual contributes, vanishing where
the units coincide, which provides a built-in control; against a forecast
frozen before an external corpus of $187$ hospitals it reproduces in shape but
not magnitude. Fusion is conditional on balance between streams: worth
$0.0370$ on one cohort, untestable on a second at ceiling, and null on a third
until its streams are equalized. An
individual-level membership-inference audit with a passing positive control
refutes the predicted leakage difference without establishing equivalence.
\end{abstract}
